# Appendix A. Working log

Yiyang Qin | FoodyMan

## 19 October 2026 — W10 and W11: Method design and evidence selection

Of the six systemic issues recorded in A2, I started with coordination after a report receives no response. As the prototype designer, I wanted to examine the limits of making a problem visible and leaving housemates to volunteer to handle it, before assuming that penalties or compulsory allocation were needed.

I compared social practice theory, COM-B and value-sensitive design as three analytical perspectives. For each, I considered the same evidence route: A2 materials, relevant research, scenarios that develop over time, and individual interviews. I initially chose social practice theory because it could examine whether tools, skills and shared understandings came together as a workable coordination process. COM-B more directly addressed the conditions for action, while value-sensitive design foregrounded fairness, autonomy and pressure.

The four proposed criteria were to distinguish reasons for silence and alternative explanations; identify who notices, responds, takes responsibility and follows up; ground future judgements in evidence and reasons for change; and remain feasible within about two weeks. In W11, I compared the evidence sources further: A2 established the design's boundaries, literature offered comparable mechanisms, and interviews could provide experiences and expectations of shared fridges. I could initially approach two housemates in the same residence. A short deployment might not encounter a meaningful unanswered report, so I retained scenario interviews and document analysis as alternatives.

## 23 October 2026 — W12: Mechanism and criteria before the run

The question shifted towards why food might remain stored for a long time even when ownership, location and permission to take it are clear. My own experience suggested that permission to take food and permission to discard it needed to be separated. I also needed to examine concerns about the last portion, food belonging to someone else, and time in storage. COM-B offered a way to distinguish information needs, social permission, expected consequences and immediate responses.

I planned two or three individual interviews to examine the explanation, without requiring participants to live together. The criteria were to cover COM-B and identify gaps; explain earlier decisions and later consequences; challenge the proposed loop and retain alternative causes; and ground recommendations in who would act and what burdens they would carry. The strongest expected claim was to identify plausible mechanisms of concern and possible preventive conditions in these cases. A mechanism diagram would describe an explanation rather than establish causation.

## 28 October 2026 — W13: Scenario adjustments and three interviews

I made scenario-based interviewing the main method, with a bad ending as one follow-up question and COM-B guiding additional questions and analysis. Owner and housemate roles were compared only in the last-portion scenario. Time could move forward without a fixed future date, and participants could say that nothing changed or that the food had already been dealt with. The first criterion was refined to explaining choices and concerns. The other three continued to address changes over time, alternative explanations and grounded recommendations.

Three participants completed interviews that day and were coded P01–P03. The recordings lasted approximately 20 minutes, 17 minutes 30 seconds, and 16 minutes 6 seconds. The interviews covered general sharing, the last portion, continued storage and dealing with food when moving out. Joint purchase appeared in additional scenarios for P01 and P03; P02 mainly discussed individually owned food offered for sharing. The condition explicitly allowing someone to take the last portion without asking was completed only with P03. The storage-information comparison was not completed.

## 29 October 2026 — W13: Joint purchase and relationships

Based on the interviews and my own judgement, I focused the inquiry on how consideration for one another and shared rights affect decisions to use, leave or discard jointly purchased food. I selected Layer 3.1. Here, AA means sharing the cost of food, not sharing rent. Continued storage and waste became possible consequences, with 4.2 no longer the main layer.

I updated the roadmap's question, mechanism, scenarios and criteria to distinguish taking food, taking the last portion and discarding it, while allowing for cases without hesitation. The revised criteria would help a subsequent round of research. Evaluation of this run would still return to the 23 October record and the 28 October interview guide to explain what the earlier criteria actually produced.

## 7 November 2026 — Analysis and report planning

Comparison of the three transcripts did not support a necessary link between interpersonal concerns and waste. P01 would leave a portion for someone else but end the wait by asking directly. P02 tended to take food in a larger sharing group without asking everyone individually. P03 located reluctance to ask more specifically in relationships involving conflict. Longer storage did not increase hesitation for all three participants.

The method produced specific judgements and useful counterexamples. It provided weaker coverage of jointly purchased food and of the practical costs of recommendations and coordination. I therefore narrowed the recommendation from adding stronger handling mechanisms to the website to comparing shared agreements and the scope of last-portion permission, while observing who carries the communication and maintenance work.

I chose a methodological case study, initially addressed to Peter Worthy at UQ DECO6500. A pilot evaluation report could foreground preliminary findings, while an evaluation handover brief could foreground implementation of the next round. This project's main value lay in how scenarios revealed boundaries of authority and how prompts shaped the evidence, so I selected the methodological case study. The Chinese draft and evidence index were completed on 7 November. I planned personal review and English finalisation for 8 November, submission before 14:00 on 9 November with a saved receipt, and separate preparation for the critical discussion.

## 7 November 2026 — Added: Effort in shared decisions and reflection on the method

I developed the research question to ask: When people jointly buy and share food, how do consideration for one another and the effort of coordination affect decisions to use it, leave it, discard it or defer deciding? AA still means sharing the cost of food, and Layer 3.1 remains the main layer. I retained interview accounts in which asking resolved the issue, participants took food directly, or time did not change their choice.

I also reflected on taking part in P01's three-day shared-food group-chat simulation. All three interview participants took part. Discussions sometimes stalled and required the facilitator to move the group to the next scenario. On 5 November, P01 commented that people might defer deciding when there was no requirement to decide immediately. This unrecorded comment and my participation experience helped me consider why individual interviews so readily produced definite answers. P01 retains the complete group-chat material.

The revised explanation separates knowing how to ask from being willing to initiate and sustain the discussion. When the food has little appeal and there is little urgency to deal with it, confirming shares, waiting for replies and reaching agreement may make deferral more attractive. Literature helped me consider convenience and priorities, cognitive effort, differences between evidence-gathering methods, and shared food practices. A subsequent method should include an explicit option to leave the matter unresolved and observe who initiates, responds and moves the decision forward.

I changed the reader to members of the FoodyMan project team responsible for further design and evaluation, replacing the individual reader used in draft v1. I completed Chinese draft v2, the evidence and method evaluation document, and the working log, preserving v1 and the criteria set before the interviews. Personal listening checks, English finalisation, submission and the critical discussion remained to be completed.
