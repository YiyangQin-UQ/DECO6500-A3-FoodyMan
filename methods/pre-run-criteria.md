# Method criteria and strongest-claim history

The criteria below preserve the dated sequence of the method design. The 23 October record sets the initial yardstick; the 28 October guide revises its use before the reported interviews. The 29 October and 7 November refinements belong to the subsequent analysis and next-method design. The three interviews took place on 28 October 2026.

<a id="criteria-23-oct"></a>
## 23 October 2026 — initial pre-run record

**Source:** *A3 Working Log: Method evaluation criteria before the run*, dated 23 October 2026. The following criteria and strongest-claim wording are reproduced from that record.

The plan was a small pilot of 2–3 individual interviews with students who had shared-fridge experience. Participants did not need to live together. It described COM-B-guided future pre-mortem interviews about food remaining unused and being discarded despite clear information and permission. The reinforcing loop was a proposed explanation to examine.

### C1 — Cover COM-B

> Cover COM-B, which I will know because each interview documents all components, including absent barriers or gaps.

Evidence to retain: Record each COM-B component: physical and psychological capability; physical and social opportunity; reflective and automatic motivation. For each, keep a supporting response, an explicit indication of no barrier, or an unresolved gap. Missing answers are gaps, not evidence of no barrier.

### C2 — Explore change over time

> Explore change over time, which I will know because each participant explains an earlier decision and a later consequence.

Evidence to retain: Keep a participant-explained sequence connecting an earlier decision, continued storage and a later consequence. Record how the basis for judgment changes over time, rather than just changing dates.

### C3 — Challenge the loop

> Challenge the loop, which I will know because notes record alternatives and supporting or conflicting reasons, without requiring agreement.

Evidence to retain: Ask openly before showing the proposed loop. Ask for other explanations and conditions where the loop would not occur. Record agreement, disagreement or inability to judge and the reason. Do not require a counterexample or consensus for the method to count as working.

### C4 — Ground recommendations

> Ground recommendations, which I will know because each proposed change identifies an actor, rationale and burden, linked to an account.

Evidence to retain: For each recommendation retained, trace it to an interview passage or recalled case and record who would act, what they would do, why it might help, and what burden or trade-off it might introduce. A lack of grounded recommendations remains an outcome to report.

<a id="strongest-claim-23-oct"></a>
### Strongest claim if the method works as planned

> Identify plausible hesitation mechanisms and prevention conditions in 2-3 cases; not establish causality, prevalence, effectiveness or actual waste reduction.

The original record further described a context-specific account of participants' explanations, anticipated consequences and candidate prevention conditions. It excluded validating the causal loop, estimating population patterns, establishing saturation, and demonstrating practical waste reduction. If the interviews did not elicit usable evidence, the claim would narrow accordingly.

The planned evaluation was to judge each criterion as met, partly met, not met or not yet, using interview evidence and retaining differences between participants. Completing interviews or obtaining agreement alone would not meet a criterion.

## 28 October 2026 — interview-guide revision

**Sources:** *A3 方法调整记录*, dated 28 October 2026, and [*FoodyMan 共享食物访谈提纲*, version 2.0](interview-guide-zh.md), also dated 28 October 2026. At the time of the adjustment record, actual running had not yet been reported.

The main method became individual scenario-based interviewing. The negative ending became one later development in the scenario. COM-B remained available for follow-up questions and analysis, with no compulsory six-category questioning round. Owner/housemate role comparison was confined to the last-portion scenario. One continuation in time was retained, allowing change, no change or interruption, without a fixed future date.

C1 changed from comprehensive COM-B coverage to explaining decisions and concerns, with framework categories used when relevant. The other criteria and the small-sample claim boundary were retained. The four criteria in the Chinese guide were:

| Criterion | Original Chinese wording from the 28 October guide |
| --- | --- |
| C1 — Explain decisions and concerns | 获得具体选择及其理由，能区分想吃与实际取用、主人与舍友的不同判断；按相关性分析COM-B因素并记录缺口。 |
| C2 — Understand change over time | 能比较初始与继续存放后的判断，指出改变、不变或中断的条件及理由。 |
| C3 — Check the loop and alternative explanations | 能记录支持、反对或修正回路的回答，以及其他原因；区分主动提出和提示后回答。 |
| C4 — Ground suggestions and identify who acts | 建议对应具体回答，说明谁何时做什么、为何可能有用及可能负担；识别未解决问题。 |

In English, these require specific choices and reasons; comparison of initial and later decisions; supporting, conflicting and alternative explanations with prompt effects retained; and recommendations connected to evidence, actors, timing and burdens. COM-B gaps remain visible where relevant.

The guide's claim boundary reads: “小样本最多支持这些参与者对可能机制及改变条件的解释；不能证明回路的因果关系、普遍性、网站有效性或现实减废效果。” It therefore retains the 23 October ambition to identify plausible mechanisms and conditions in these cases, rather than establishing causal or practical effects.

## 29 October and 7 November — later refinements

**29 October source:** *A3 共同采购方法标准修订 v2*, dated 29 October 2026. After the three tests, the roadmap focused on jointly bought food and Layer 3.1. Its revised criteria asked whether the method could distinguish eating, taking the last portion and disposal; explain interpersonal concerns or other reasons; explain whether time or explicit agreements changed choices; and ground recommendations in evidence, actors, required consent and burdens. Its strongest claim concerned participants' understanding of shared-food rights, mutual concerns and possible coordination needs. This revision was stored separately from the original pre-run standards.

**7 November source:** Chinese draft v2, its evidence-and-method evaluation, and the [participation reflection](../analysis/participation-reflection.md). The analysis developed the distinction between knowing that one could ask and investing effort in initiating, waiting for and advancing a shared decision. These refinements guide a future run: include a genuine option to defer, track who initiates and responds, and examine how a decision progresses. They supplement the interpretation of the existing interviews; the evaluation of the completed run still returns to the 23 and 28 October criteria above.
