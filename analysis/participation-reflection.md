# Participation reflection: advancing a shared decision

**Prepared:** 7 November 2026. **Source:** the A3 researcher's first-hand participation and subsequent recollection, recorded during preparation of Chinese draft v2.

The researcher participated in a three-day shared-food group-chat simulation facilitated by P01. All three A3 interview participants took part in that simulation. The complete group-chat records belong to P01's separate project and remain with P01. This account uses the researcher's participation experience and recollection of a later conversation; it is not an export or reanalysis of the peer project's messages. The precise start and end dates of the three-day simulation were not established in the A3 source record.

<a id="ref-g01"></a>
## REF-G01 — first-hand experience of stalled discussion

During the simulation, discussion sometimes stalled in waiting and postponed decisions. The facilitator had to advance the next scenario to move the process forward. The researcher experienced this as difficulty progressing a decision through several people's responses and reaching consensus.

This experience makes the work of prompting, waiting and moving a discussion forward visible. In the individual interviews, a participant could say that asking the other person would resolve uncertainty; in the continuing group discussion, somebody still had to initiate and sustain that process. The relevant methodological contrast is between explaining a feasible action and seeing whether discussion advances when an immediate answer is not continuously requested.

<a id="ref-f01"></a>
## REF-F01 — P01's later comment

On 5 November 2026, during a later conversation, P01 commented that people may leave a decision open when there is no immediate requirement to decide. This is the researcher's paraphrase of an unrecorded comment, written down on 7 November, rather than a verbatim quotation.

The comment helped the researcher interpret the stalled discussion and reconsider the interview setting: repeated questions support clear answers, while an ongoing group interaction allows silence, waiting and deferral to occur. The next method should therefore make “not deciding yet” an explicit option and record who takes responsibility for restarting the decision.

## Relationship to the interview evidence

These reflections are supplementary sources for method evaluation. The main interview corpus remains the three individual interviews of 28 October, indexed separately in the [22-item evidence index](evidence-index.md). P01's account that asking the next day prevented reserved food from becoming waste remains in [P01-E07](evidence-index.md#p01-e07). P02's unwillingness to ask five or six people individually remains in [P02-E04](evidence-index.md#p02-e04).

Together, these materials motivate an explanation centred on the investment needed to coordinate: knowing whom to ask, initiating contact, waiting for replies and moving toward an outcome. The group-chat experience supplies an observed process within a simulation; food-waste consequences remain a question for subsequent household investigation. “Difficulty advancing a multi-person decision” describes this experience without treating participant count as a tested causal variable.
