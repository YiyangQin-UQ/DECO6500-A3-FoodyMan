## P01-E04 — Joint rights and shares
**Source:** [P01, 07:55–09:21](../transcripts/P01.md#p01-e04). **Material:** Scenario response with an added joint-purchase prompt.

An individual owner retained the right to take their own food. For jointly paid food, P01 would consider the number already eaten: having eaten relatively few could justify taking the last one; roughly equal shares prompted a question. Being asked felt respectful, although direct taking could also be acceptable.

**Reading note:** The interviewer introduced joint payment. One participant explained both roles.

## P01-E05 — Storage and disposal authority
**Source:** [P01, 12:49–14:56](../transcripts/P01.md#p01-e05). **Material:** Scenario response.

P01 would ask about the owner’s plans and whether food could be discarded. Knowing food remained available for future use felt different from unexpectedly rediscovering forgotten food. Jointly paid food also required asking before disposal.

**Reading note:** The distinction concerns continuing awareness versus rediscovery, rather than calendar duration alone.

Over-purchasing, coursework, less cooking and priority for newly bought food could leave older food unused. Buying less and checking the fridge before shopping were suggested.

## P01-E07 — Counterexample: communication resolves waiting

The interviewer proposed a full story in which both joint purchasers reserved food for each other and it was wasted. P01 recognised leaving a share, but said it had not reached the point of waste: after a day, P01 would ask whether the other person still wanted it.

## P01-E08 — Triggers for disposal or asking
**Source:** [P01, 19:06–19:45](../transcripts/P01.md#p01-e08). **Material:** Scenario response.

P01 considered very long storage or moving out reasons for direct disposal. Asking remained relevant when food was still wanted, belonged to someone else or was jointly purchased, and taking it would leave none for the other person.

**Reading note:** This contextual disposal threshold differs from the earlier ask-first account; both are retained.

## P02-E01 — Contrasting household practice
**Source:** [P02, 00:36–03:07](../transcripts/P02.md#p02-e01). **Material:** Recalled experience.

Housemates generally purchased food separately and sometimes shared surplus ingredients. Frozen food could fill the freezer. Joint buying sometimes involved two or three people, rather than the whole five- or six-person household.

**Reading note:** P02 corrected the interviewer’s assumption that frozen food caused no space problem. Detailed joint-purchase incidents were not elicited.

## P02-E03 — Different decisions and prompt effects
**Source:** [P02, 06:33–08:13](../transcripts/P02.md#p02-e03). **Material:** Scenario response with successive added conditions.

With one meal left, P02 first said they would take it all. If others had already taken some, no further portion needed reserving. After the interviewer added that the owner had not taken any, P02 said they would check with the owner.

**Reading note:** The later answer follows a changed scenario; it does not replace the earlier willingness to take the food.

## P02-E04 — Counterexample: coordination effort
**Source:** [P02, 10:02–11:54](../transcripts/P02.md#p02-e04). **Material:** Scenario response and experience clarification.

P02 would take the final ice cream and would not ask five or six people individually, assuming others had had an opportunity. P02 rarely encountered this exact last-portion situation and thought an owner offering food should be ready for it to be finished.

**Reading note:** The response distinguishes the cost of asking from permission and records the limited matching real experience.
