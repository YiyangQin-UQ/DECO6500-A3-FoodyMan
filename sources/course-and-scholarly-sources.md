## Scholarly sources and their role

1. Michie, van Stralen and West (2011). [The behaviour change wheel](https://doi.org/10.1186/1748-5908-6-42). COM-B definitions and action conditions.
2. Manika, Iacovidou, Canhoto, Pei and Mach (2022). [Capabilities, opportunities and motivations that drive food waste disposal practices](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jclepro.2022.133449). Example of applying COM-B to disposal practices; distinct from jointly purchased-food decisions.
3. Graham-Rowe, Jessop and Sparks (2014). [Identifying motivations and barriers to minimising household food waste](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.resconrec.2013.12.005). Convenience and priorities; publisher abstract and accessible extracts consulted.
4. Westbrook, Kester and Braver (2013). [What is the subjective cost of cognitive effort](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0068210). Cognitive effort and subjective reward value; relevant full text consulted. Its cognitive-task experiment helps formulate the coordination-effort interpretation.
5. Jones-Garcia, Bakalis and Flintham (2022). [Consumer behaviour and food waste](https://doi.org/10.3390/foods11142048). Interview accounts and technology-probe records; [full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9324548/) consulted.
6. Morone, Falcone, Imbert and Morone (2018). [Does food sharing lead to food waste reduction](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jclepro.2018.01.208). Collaboration conditions in joint purchasing, cooking and eating; publisher abstract and accessible extracts consulted.
